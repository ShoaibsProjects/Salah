# F2-TZ2 — match solar cycles to a requested local date

**Status:** F2-TZ2 is implemented in the current working tree and awaits architect review. F2-TZ1 is reviewed and committed as `6d5341c`. Phase 1 and Phase 2 gates remain open; this remains a six-zone research slice.

F2-TZ3 adds the next bounded layer: localize every event in each selected cycle. See [local schedule slice](civil-time-local-schedule.md) and [schedule contract](../specification/local-prayer-schedule-contract-v0.1.md). Transit selection semantics remain unchanged.

F2-TZ4 independently classifies whether the civil date exists under the pinned zone transitions; this prevents `Zero` transit matches from being mistaken for a skipped date. See [civil-date existence contract](../specification/civil-date-existence-contract-v0.1.md).

F2-TZ5 expands the accepted zone byte set to the 598-name IANA pack without changing this selector's enumeration policy. See the [v0.2 scope amendment](../specification/civil-date-transit-selector-v0.2.md) and [pack contract](../specification/global-timezone-pack-v0.1.md); this original packet remains the six-zone evidence record.

## User-visible question

For a chosen location, IANA zone, and Gregorian date, which calculated solar cycle has its upper transit on that **local date**? The current core can calculate a cycle from a UTC anchor, and `salah-time` can convert a UTC instant under six pinned 2026d zone files. F2-TZ2 joins those operations without passing a time-zone offset into the legacy fixed-offset API.

The selector must return every matching cycle. A local date can have zero, one, or more matches because civil clocks and dates can jump. `Zero` means **no matching transit**; it must not by itself be labeled a skipped civil date. The already sourced Apia 2011 transition is a separate example where the date itself was skipped. UTC-to-local conversion of a transit is unique even when a local wall hour is repeated.

## Scope and result contract

- Implement the selector in `salah-time`, leaving `salah-core` and its F2-TZ1 contract unchanged. Input: validated `CivilDate` and `Coordinates`, caller-chosen supported canonical zone ID and its exact pinned TZif bytes, `MethodProfile`, and `AsrCriterion`. No coordinate-to-zone inference.
- Return a typed `Zero | One | Multiple` result. Every candidate carries the full `UtcAnchorTimes`, the transit's `LocalCivilTime`, the requested date, zone ID, tzdb version, and a new selector-policy ID/revision. Candidates are unique and sorted by transit UTC. Method-adjusted Dhuhr must not be used for date membership; use the **rounded, unadjusted `selected_transit` UTC instant** because it is the versioned core's second-precision event. Record that near a civil midnight, rounding can affect membership by at most one second.
- Keep `Event::Unavailable` and all UTC event records intact. Converting every prayer event to a local display, minute rounding, notification instants, high-latitude alternatives, and consumer wording are later steps.
- Preserve the existing six-zone allowlist, exact-byte/version check, and no-host/no-network behavior. Make malformed bytes, unsupported zone/date, arithmetic failure, or an incomplete enumeration explicit errors. Never turn such a failure into `Zero`.

## Exhaustive search requirement

The implementation must justify coverage, not sample one local noon and assume there is one transit.

1. Let `D0` be requested Gregorian midnight interpreted only as a **numeric UTC reference**, not as the zone's actual local midnight. Jiff's supported offset range is −25:59:59 through +25:59:59. Therefore a UTC second whose local date is `D` must lie in the inclusive interval `[D0 − 93_599, D0 + 86_399 + 93_599]`. Use checked integer arithmetic. Filter a candidate transit by this interval before its local conversion.
2. Intersect that interval with the public F2-TZ1 anchor range and sample anchors at intervals of at most six hours, including both ends. Recalculate each distinct transit with a canonical in-range anchor near that transit before recording its cycle. Deduplicate on the rounded unadjusted transit UTC second; retain the raw transit in the core record. Convert the canonical transit through the **same** supplied zone bytes and keep it only when its returned local date equals `D`.
3. Document why this scan reaches every transit that could match within the supported 1900–2100 dates. The NOAA-Meeus equation-of-time expression in `solar.rs` admits a conservative absolute bound of one hour over this range: for the relevant Julian centuries, `|eccentricity| < 0.017`, `|obliquity| < 24°`, and `y = tan(obliquity/2)^2 < 0.046`; bounding each sine/cosine term by one makes the expression's bracket less than `0.085` radians, or less than 20 minutes after the `4 × degrees` conversion. Recheck and derive this bound in the new specification. The selector's index is `round((A + E(A) + longitude_seconds)/86_400 − 0.5)`. The eight-iteration transit returned by F2-TZ1 is within two hours of its exact fixed point because the difference of any two bounded equation-of-time values is less than two hours. Therefore an anchor within six hours of the returned transit is within eight hours of the exact transit; adding at most two hours for `E(A)−E(t)` keeps the index operand within ten hours of the half-integer boundary, less than twelve hours. A six-hour grid has a point within three hours of any transit inside its endpoints. At the earliest and latest supported dates, clipping to F2-TZ1's anchor range leaves at most two hours between an extreme possible matching transit and the nearest endpoint. If any part of this argument fails under the implementation's actual arithmetic or range, return an explicit coverage error or stop with the counterexample; do not report a falsely complete result.
4. The transit may lie just outside the F2-TZ1 **anchor** range at a boundary date, while a nearby in-range anchor still selects it. If a nonmatching candidate converts to a local date outside `CivilDate`'s 1900–2100 range, it cannot match the validated requested date; handle that case explicitly without hiding malformed data or a failed conversion.

## Evidence and review gate

Use the pinned zone pack to cover normal Chicago, its 2026 spring and fall transitions, London transition, Kathmandu +05:45, Kiritimati +14 date carriage, and Apia 2011-12-30 (zero matches, with the independent TZif transition evidence for its skipped date). Deliberately pair a longitude near 97.5°E with `America/Chicago`: solar transit then falls near local midnight, so the 23-hour spring date is a candidate for **zero matches while the date exists**, and the 25-hour fall date is a candidate for **two matches**. Derive the exact outcomes from the kernel and pinned zone pack; adjust the longitude if necessary, and do not invent clock values. Include the earliest and latest supported dates and a case near an anchor-selection boundary. For every returned candidate, independently reconvert its transit and confirm the date, zone, data version, ordering, and retained method/model provenance. Test invalid zone/bytes and ensure they cannot yield `Zero`.

The review gate is a reproducible, complete enumeration for the six pinned zones with typed zero/one/multiple outcomes, unchanged core/CLI behavior, and an honest limitation statement. It does not approve global zone coverage, a regional calculation method, or a consumer prayer timetable.
